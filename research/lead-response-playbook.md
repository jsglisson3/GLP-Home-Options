## The promise to a homeowner

Many visitors are older, short on time or money, and wary of "we buy houses" ads. For them, the first reply is the proof that the site is real. The goal is simple: **a real person answers every call, text and form, the same day, and the first conversation is about their situation, not our offer.**

## 1. Look before building (30 minutes, in GoHighLevel)

Write down what already exists, so we change as little as possible:

- What happens when the contact form is submitted (which workflow, which tags, which pipeline stage)?
- Who is told, and how: text, email, app notification? Do **both** Joey and Ethan get it?
- Is there an automatic reply to the visitor? What does it say, and does it name GLP and offer STOP?
- Is a missed-call text-back on? From which number? Is a text campaign registered, and under which website address?
- Is the "Book a call with us" calendar (linked from Contact) connected to the same pipeline?

## 2. Instant alert to Joey and Ethan

When the form is submitted, both founders get a text and an email with: name, phone, email, what the person wrote, the page they came from, and the time. Whoever sees it first replies in the thread ("I've got this") so the other does not double-call. If nobody has marked it handled after a set time (Joey decides how long), the alert repeats.

## 3. Instant reply to the visitor (draft, for Joey to edit)

Keep it informational: say we got it, who will call, and when. Offers and pricing do not belong in an automatic message. Include the business name and the way to stop messages.

> Hi [first name], this is GLP Home Options. Thanks for reaching out about your home. Joey or Ethan will call you [today / by tomorrow morning]. Need us sooner? Call or text [main phone]. Reply STOP to stop texts.

**Before this goes live:** the form's consent wording and the carrier registration must be settled (see section 6 of `compliance-questions-for-joey.md`), and sends should stay between 8 a.m. and 9 p.m. in the person's time zone.

## 4. Missed-call text-back (draft)

If a call goes unanswered, a short text goes to the caller. Same rules as above (name, how to stop, a person will follow up). GoHighLevel's own page does not say whether it adds opt-out language or respects time of day, so check that in the account before turning it on.

> Sorry we missed your call. This is GLP Home Options. Joey or Ethan will call you back [today]. Reply STOP to stop texts.

## 5. The first call (guide, not a script)

A good first call is mostly listening. Plain English, no pressure, and no promises about price on the phone.

1. Thank them, say who is calling and that the first conversation is free (Joey must confirm that line).
2. Ask what is going on and what they are hoping for. Let them finish.
3. Ask about timing, the condition of the house, what is owed (only what they volunteer), and who else is involved (family, an heir, a co-owner).
4. Say the three paths in plain words: sell to GLP, list with an agent, or keep the house. Say honestly when listing or keeping is the better fit.
5. Offer to run their numbers side by side, the way the net sheet on the site does, and agree the next step and the day. Then do it.
6. Write down the notes the same day (see section 7).

If the person is under pressure from a foreclosure or a deadline, do not promise to "stop" anything; say what the options are and suggest they talk to a housing counselor or attorney as well. Joey should ask an attorney how Tennessee's rules for foreclosure-related services apply (section 3 of the compliance notes).

## 6. Follow-up if there is no answer

A simple rhythm, all by phone, text or email within the allowed hours, stopping the moment someone says no or STOP:

- Same day: call, then a short text.
- Next day: call.
- Day 3: a short email or text with one useful thing (for example, the link to the net sheet).
- Day 7: one last friendly note, then stop. No endless drip.

Joey and Ethan may prefer different timing. The point is that every person gets a clear, polite ending, not silence.

## 7. What to write down, every time

Date and time of the inquiry, how they found us, who called, when, what was said, what each side agreed to do next, and whether they consented to texts and how. These notes are also the records that back the claims on the site (for example, how long closings really take). Keep them in GoHighLevel, not in a personal phone.

## 8. Once a month, ten minutes

Count: inquiries, how many were answered the same day, how many became a real conversation, and how many ended in a plan. These are for Joey and Ethan to learn from; they are not public numbers.
